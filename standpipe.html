Overview

What Is a Standpipe System?

Standpipe systems are an essential part of a building's fire protection system, providing fire departments with immediate water supply for fire suppression — directly inside the building where it's needed most.

System Types

Wet vs Dry

Wet Standpipe

Pipes filled with water at all times
  • For buildings with heated areas
  • Ready for immediate use
  • Faster water delivery
  • Lower maintenance risk from freezing
  • Most common type in the Philippines
VS

Dry Standpipe

Pipes filled with air or nitrogen
  • For buildings with unheated areas (cold climates)
  • Water enters when a connection is opened
  • Equipped with dry alarm valve & air compressor
  • Prevents pipes from freezing
  • Required for exposed/cold environments
Compliance

Governing Codes & Standards

Redflame designs and installs standpipe systems fully compliant with all applicable Philippine laws and international fire protection standards.

Philippine Laws

  • National Building Code (PD 1096)
  • RA 9514 — Fire Code of the Philippines
  • 2019 Revised IRR of RA 9514

NFPA Standards

  • NFPA 14 — Standpipe and Hose Systems
  • NFPA 24 — Private Fire Service Mains
  • NFPA 25 — Inspection, Testing & Maintenance

Fire Code Requirements

  • Required in all buildings 15 meters (49 ft) or higher
  • Measured from lowest BFP access to highest floor
  • 2½" (65mm) hose connections at each floor level
  • Fire Department Connection (FDC) at approved location
  • Installed per NFPA 14 and accepted engineering practice

Riser Pipe

Vertical pipe running through the building's floors, carrying water from the ground-level supply up to each floor's hose connection.

Hose Connection

2½" (65mm) outlet at each floor level where fire hoses are connected. Provides firefighters direct water access on every floor.

Fire Hose Cabinet

Wall-mounted cabinet housing the fire hose and nozzle at each floor. Provides occupants and firefighters easy access to hose equipment.

Control valve at each floor's hose connection point. Opens to release pressurized water into the hose — allows controlled water flow during firefighting.
